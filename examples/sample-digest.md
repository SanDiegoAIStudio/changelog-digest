# A frost date moved the peas, then the oven learned to wait

Today's most interesting change was the same idea in two places. The seed calendar shifts sowing dates when the last frost moves, and the oven timer pauses when the lid opens.

## The story

Morning was garden-planner, session Seed calendar. Sowing dates had stayed pinned to a week the frost had left behind. The last frost date is stored once. When it moves, the early peas move with it, and later crops keep their spacing. A late cold snap had wiped out an early sowing the old calendar would not move.

Afternoon was recipe-box, session Oven timer. The countdown pauses while the lid is up and resumes when the lid shuts. A short bump no longer freezes the timer. The beep no longer fires into an open oven, where it is easy to miss.

## Breakthroughs

One stored frost date can shift the sowing list without editing each crop. The peas take the move, and later crops keep the gaps behind them.

The other find is the short bump on the lid. Treating every flicker as a real opening would stick the pause at the worst moment. A brief bump is ignored, and a real check still waits.

## Challenges

Sliding every crop by the full frost shift would pile late plantings at the end of the season. Later crops stay spaced behind the peas, but a crop with no place in that line can still land wrong. The frost date should sit beside the crop it moved.

The lid sensor can chatter. If the short bump rule gets lost, chatter becomes a stuck pause. A paused timer also looks broken when nothing shows why it stopped.

## What is next

Tomorrow the frost date should show beside each crop it moved, and the timer should mark when a pause ends.

## Video ideas

1. Store one frost date and let a seed calendar rewrite its sowing list.
2. An oven timer that pauses for an open lid, and why a short bump must not count.
3. A garden tool and a kitchen tool that got better when they learned to wait.
